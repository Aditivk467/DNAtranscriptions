Why GC percentage value?

Guanine and Cytosine form three hydrogen bonds unlike Adenine and Thymine that form only two. The GC content shows how stable the DNA structure is due to the strong bonds formed.DNA with more GC have a higher 'melting temperature' which is needed for them to separate. Different organisms have different unique average amounts of GC, this is used to help researchers study evolution.

The Central Dogma

The theory stating that genetic information flows in one direction only.

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

DNA is the permanent storage for genetic information. It exists as a double helix structure—two complementary strands woven together. DNA is tightly wrapped around proteins known as histone proteins forming a chromatin.

A Adenine pairs with Thymine
T Thymine pairs with Adenine
G Guanine pairs with Cytosine
C Cytosine pairs with Guanine

Step 2 : The Copy

Transcription

Transcription occurs inside the nucleus. DNA helicase-an enzyme, breaks the hydrogen bonds between the complementary base pairs in a specific gene causing the DNA to unwind and expose the bases. Only one of the two strands are used, this strand is known as the Template strand. This strand acts as a guide for the building of the RNA strand. Free RNA nucleotides form hydrogen bonds to their complementary exposed bases on the DNA strand. The RNA polymerase joins adjacent RNA nucleotides through phosphodiester bonds in a condensation reaction. Once the entire gene is copied the mRNA ( messenger RNA ) detaches and the DNA strands reform their double helix structure. The mRNA leaves the nucleus through the nuclear pores. The mRNA is made up of triplets known as codons


The Uracil Rule

RNA is similar to DNA, but it uses Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T). When RNA Polymerase reads an 'A' on the DNA template, it places a 'U' in the mRNA strand.


Step 3 : The Assembly

Translation

Translation occurs in the cytoplasm.The mRNA attaches to the ‘large ribosome subunit’ at the START codon. A tRNA with complementary anticodons and methionine,( an essential amino acid that the human body cannot make on its own which is used to initialize the process of making new proteins). A second tRNA binds to the next codon,bringing another amino acid causing a peptide bond to form between the two amino acids present using ATP and a ribosome bound enzyme. The ribosome moves along the mRNA and releases the first tRNA. This cycle repeats to form a chain.Once a STOP codon is encountered the translation ends.

DNA mutations

A mutation is a change in the base sequence of a DNA molecule which can result in an altered polypeptide.

Base Substitution

When a base in the DNA sequence is randomly substituted for a different base. There are three possible types of base substitution mutations:


Silent mutations: where the mutation will not alter the amino acid sequence as the genetic code is degenerate.


Missense mutations: where a single amino acid is altered in the polypeptide chain.


Nonsense mutations: where a premature STOP codon is created leading to the production of an incomplete polypeptide chain.

Base Deletion

When a nucleotide is randomly deleted from the DNA sequence. This changes the amino acid formed that the original sequence had coded for. This mutation causes a Frame-Shift by shifting the reading frame of triplets,causing an alteration downstream to all triplets on the DNA sequence.


Reading Frame

A way to divide codons that codes for an amino acid on the mRNA. An open reading frame starts with a start codon and ends the polypeptide chain when a stop codon is present.

Base Addition

Also known as insertion mutation. When a nucleotide consisting of a new base is randomly inserted into the DNA sequence. This causes a Frame-Shift mutation as well as it shifts the reading frame of triplets in the DNA sequence leading to an alteration to all downstream triplets changing the amino acid the original DNA sequence had coded for.

Eukaryotes vs Prokaryotes

One difference between Eukaryotes and Prokaryotes in protein synthesis is a process known as Splicing.

Splicing

In Eukaryotes transcription creates pre-mRNA which contains coding regions and non-coding regions. The coding regions are known as Exons while the non-coding regions are known as Introns.The pre-mRNA is spliced, where the introns are removed and exons are joined together. Bacterial DNA does not consist of introns hence they do not need to undergo splicing.
