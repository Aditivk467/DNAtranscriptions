The Central Dogma

The molecular biological framework tracking information flow from template blueprints to physical mechanics.

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

DNA is the permanent storage for genetic information. It exists as a double helix—two complementary strands woven together.

A Adenine pairs with Thymine
T Thymine pairs with Adenine
G Guanine pairs with Cytosine
C Cytosine pairs with Guanine

Step 2 : The Copy

Transcription

DNA cannot leave the nucleus. Instead, an enzyme called RNA Polymerase unzips the DNA and creates a working copy called messenger RNA (mRNA).


The Uracil Rule

RNA is similar to DNA, but it uses Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T). When RNA Polymerase reads an 'A' on the DNA template, it places a 'U' in the mRNA strand.


Step 3 : The Assembly

Translation

The mRNA travels to a ribosome, the cellular factory. The ribosome reads the mRNA sequence three letters at a time. These three-letter words are called Codons. Each codon corresponds to exactly one amino acid. As the ribosome reads the codons, it strings the amino acids together into a polypeptide chain—a protein.
